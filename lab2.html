Lab 2: Authentication vs. authorization

Authentication (AuthN) answers who are you? Authorization (AuthZ) answers what are you allowed to do? AuthN always comes first, because a system cannot decide what you may do until it knows who you are.

Comparison

Authentication (AuthN)Authorization (AuthZ)
QuestionWho are you?What may you do?
ChecksCredentials: password, code, fingerprintRoles, permissions, ownership
WhenAt sign-inOn every action, after sign-in
If it fails401 Unauthorized: identity not proven403 Forbidden: identity known, action not allowed
ExampleA student signs in with email and passwordThat student cannot create a class
In my systemSupabase Auth (signInWithPassword, a signed token)The role on each profile, RLS policies such as owns_class(), and route guards
Both demos run in your browser to show the idea. Real checks must happen on the server, because anything in browser code can be changed by the user.
